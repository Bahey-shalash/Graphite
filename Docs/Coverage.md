# Implementation coverage

What Graphite does today, how each part is verified, and what is still missing. `OBJECTIVE.md` describes the complete product; this file tracks progress toward it. Update it with every change that adds, removes, or re-verifies behavior.

Last updated: 2026-10-12.

## PDFs no longer grow with every edit (alpha only): 2026-10-12

Cherry-picked to alpha from `main-pdf-size` (`a38bce6`) to be tried on the iPad before it goes to main; main still writes the unreachable copies. It fixes the size problem found with the picture work (below).

Found on 2026-10-12: each save after moving, turning or cropping a picture on a PDF page kept the replaced stamp annotation in the file; after four edits 4.4 MB of a 5.5 MB PDF holding one photo were unreachable copies. Erased ink and removed markup grew files the same way (twenty strokes erased four times added 0.96 MB). PDFKit writes all objects of the file it read unless the page tree changes. Saves now go through `PDFObjectCompaction`, which keeps only the objects reachable from the trailer (see Save and conflict boundary in `Architecture.md`). The same pass stores a picture's appearance as its JPEG instead of the inflated pixels and opaque soft mask PDFKit wrote, and drops the second base64 copy of the picture PDFKit put in its markup data: a 187 KB JPEG took 693 KB, it now takes 438 KB (the JPEG and its base64 text for re-editing).

Checked by `PDFObjectCompactionTests` (7, on macOS with `swift test` and on the iPad simulator in `GraphiteIntegrationTests`): five moves and a turn of a photo keep the file within 4 KB of its size with the photo and store the JPEG once as the image; erased ink and removed markup leave the file within 1 KB; a hand-written PDF with an outline, page labels, a link, a filled form field, named destinations, viewer settings, document information and another application's annotation with a key of its own keeps all of them, and every catalog key, through a picture added and moved three times; files PDFKit did not write in its form are left alone; the JPEG image renders within 3 of 255 per channel of PDFKit's pixels in sRGB and Display P3. Without the compaction the size tests fail (the ink one by 0.96 MB). `PicturesAndPaperTests.testMovingAPhotoAndSavingAfterEachMoveKeepsThePDFAboutItsSize` does the same through `PDFSession` on the iPad simulator. In the iPad simulator app: a photo placed on page 1 of a 44-page, 3.7 MB lecture PDF, moved twice and turned, with autosave after each step: 5,022,273, 5,022,521, 5,022,541 and 5,019,445 bytes, no unreachable objects, the image stored as `DCTDecode`. Save time on a Mac with a 102 MB, 592-page lecture: 0.27 s before, 0.39 s after in a debug build (the compaction itself 0.05 s optimized); 30 MB, 695 pages: 0.11 s before, 0.22 s after. Not checked on the iPad, so neither its save times; Preview and other readers were not opened on the saved files (Core Graphics and PDFKit read them in the tests).

## Main merged into alpha: one rule for where a selection frame's handles stand: 2026-10-12

Main `829779d` (the embedded PDF's toolbar after Back, and every corner of a picture near the top of a PDF) merged into alpha `c693653`. Three changes to the selection frame met: main's frame kept inside the PDF view's visible part, alpha's rotate handle below a picture it cannot be reached above, and the lasso's handles (a quarter of the frame at most inward, one knob on a tiny frame, a corner handle past a page's edge stood just inside it). They are now one rule in `SelectionFrameView.layoutSubviews`, against one reachable region (the superview's bounds less its safe area, in the frame's coordinates): each corner handle is first placed for its corner with the lasso's quarter rule, then kept inside the region by main's `handleCenter` (moved along the frame's sides, never past the opposite side, knob centered once moved); the rotate handle stands below when its center above is out of the same region. The lasso's own clamp (`reachableCenter`, in the superview's coordinates) and the rotate handle's `canReachHandle` did the same job as `handleCenter` and the region, and were removed; `handleCenter` is the one kept. `staysInSuperviewSafeArea` now only decides the clipping mask and refusing touches outside the region (on for PDF pictures, off for ink, which may hang past its page). A turned frame uses the rectangle around the region in its own coordinates, exact for quarter turns and slightly generous otherwise.

Checked: `PicturesAndPaperTests` and `InkSelectionTests` hosted on an iPad Pro 11-inch (M5) simulator, iOS 27, 34 of 34, including main's `testEveryCornerHandleOfAPictureAtTheTopOfTheViewCanBeTouched`, alpha's `testTheRotateHandleStaysWhereATouchReachesItAndVoiceOverReadsTheAngle` and the lasso's `testACornerHandlePastThePagesEdgeStandsInsideItAndTakesTouches`. The whole suite and the checks by finger are recorded with the later alpha changes below.

## An embedded PDF's toolbar answers right after returning to its note: 2026-10-12

Found on 2026-10-12 in the simulator: a tap on an embedded PDF's Annotate or Open in PDF View within about a second of returning to the note was ignored. Also found, not changed: switching tabs builds a note's rendered blocks again, and SwiftUI takes about 100 to 150 ms to show them and take touches; a tap in that time is lost. While an embedded PDF opens again (it is released 1.5 s after leaving the screen), Annotate is disabled; a large PDF on an iPad may take noticeably long to open.

## Drawings in Live Preview no longer flicker: 2026-10-12

Reported by the owner with a screen recording from the iPad: while typing under the drawings at the end of a note, the drawings went black for about 75 ms, now and then; moving the cursor onto a drawing's line and off it showed a spinner and an empty space for a frame or two; scrolling showed spinners. Three causes, each reproduced in the iPad simulator with a copy of the recorded note and its six drawings, with frame recordings (`simctl io recordVideo`) and breakpoints that log:

- **Every save rebuilt every rendered block.** The system reports a note's own coordinated save back to the vault's file presenter (`VaultMonitor.presentedSubitemDidChange` for the note, seen in the debugger, in the simulator's local vault and not only in iCloud), the outside-change refresh then raises `drawingVersion`, and the editor answered that with `removeAllWidgets()`. A new embed view shows nothing until it has found its file through the index, so a second or so after each pause in typing every drawing on screen went blank (2 frames in the simulator, about 75 ms on the iPad). The views are now given the new environment in place (`LivePreviewWidgetSignature.drawingVersion`), keep what they show while they look their files up again, and a table or callout keeps its prepared text until the new one is ready. An embedded PDF, which was rebuilt the same way, now checks its file when the version changes and shows a version saved elsewhere unless it has annotations not saved yet (`EmbeddedPDFViewer.drawingVersion`; built and run in the hosted suites, but not checked by hand with a PDF annotated in another tab).
- **A new view of an embed started empty.** The pool of off-screen views keeps two drawings, so a drawing scrolled back into view, or next to a block the cursor enters, was usually made again and showed a progress indicator first. It now draws, in its first frame, the picture it last showed (`LivePreviewEmbedImageLocations` remembers where each embed's image was found; `ReadingImageCache` keeps the picture).
- **A drawing the cursor leaves was placed a turn late.** Restyling its line replaces its fragment, which TextKit had not laid out when the blocks were placed, so placement waited for the next turn of the run loop while the text below had already moved down. The viewport is now laid out in the same pass, and once more if that layout scrolled the view (a tap on a misspelled word below the drawing selects it and scrolls).

Checked in the iPad simulator ("Graphite Flicker iPad", dark appearance) by frame recordings, before and after: typing with three autosaves, before 2 blank frames at each save, after none (no frame below 80% of the drawings' area); the cursor entering and leaving a drawing by `</>` and taps, three times by a misspelled word: before 28 to 38 ms of empty space each time, after none; plain taps were clean after the change. Automated: `UiLivePreviewEmbedContinuityTests` (5, macOS), and in `LivePreviewBlockPlacementTests` `testAChangedFileKeepsTheDrawingOnScreen`, which failed with the old teardown (in a scratch copy) and passes, and `testADrawingTheCursorLeavesIsPlacedInTheSameLayoutPass`, which passes with and without the change (it does not reach the late path; the recordings are the evidence for it). Ran: the package UI tests (527, 1 skipped measurement) and the hosted `LivePreviewBlockPlacementTests`, `LivePreviewRevealTests` and `EditingContinuityTests` (27); `ReadingTasksAndEquationsTests.testEquationNumberStandsAtTheRightOfTheColumnAndIsNotCutOffWhenNarrow` fails on this simulator (iPad Pro 11-inch (M5)) with and without these changes, on main 3865bc4 alone too; it is in reading view, which they do not touch. Not checked on the iPad, in reading view (which also reloads its images when `drawingVersion` changes), or on canvases.

## Pictures on PDF pages, finished: 2026-10-12

Found in the review of the changes of 2026-10-11 (below): a picture placed on a PDF page and made smaller was smooth while it was edited but showed moiré again once the PDF was opened again, the owner's original report half unfixed.

- Cause: after a reopen PDFKit draws the stamp's saved appearance, and draws its image with the interpolation of the context it draws into. Rendered by `PDFPage.draw` into a context with no interpolation, the saved appearance of a 720-pixel picture shown 120 points wide differed from the same image drawn by Core Graphics with high interpolation by 49 gray levels (root mean square), and by 5 when the context had high interpolation too (macOS); PDFView on iOS draws it with low interpolation even with `interpolationQuality` set to high, while the picture drawn by `PDFPictureAnnotation` after an edit sets high interpolation itself. Fix: every shown PDF document makes the stamps it reads `PDFPictureAnnotation`s (`PDFPictureDrawingDelegate`), which draw Graphite's picture from its keys; the file is not changed.
- Seen in the iPad simulator (iPad Pro 11-inch (M5), iOS 27): a PDF with a zone-plate picture of 720 × 540 pixels shown about 166 points wide. Before the change, opened again, it differed from the same picture after a move and undo (drawn live) by 5.6 gray levels RMS (largest 39, 40% of pixels equal), with plain moiré rings; after the change, opened again at the same zoom, it is pixel for pixel the live-drawn picture (RMS 0, 100% equal). Opening and closing it left the file's bytes unchanged (same MD5).
- The written appearance (`/Interpolate true`, the picture's own pixels) was checked in other readers at 144 dpi against a Lanczos reduction: Quick Look 5.1 and Ghostscript 6.2 gray levels RMS, both smooth; poppler 27.7 with moiré, the same as for the same JPEG drawn straight into page content, so poppler's reduction is the cause and the writing side was left as it is. Found, not changed: PDFKit writes the appearance's image as Flate pixels with a soft mask, not the JPEG's bytes, so a photograph's appearance is several times the size of its JPEG.
- Tests: `PDFPictureTests.testAPictureOfAReopenedPDFIsDrawnAsSmoothlyAsWhileItIsEdited` (pages turned 0° and 90°, pictures turned 0 and 30°: opened again and drawn without interpolation it is within 0.5 gray levels of the live-drawn picture, while the same file without the delegate is more than 3 away; Graphite reads back the same picture) and `testPicturesOfInsertedPagesAreDrawnTooAndOtherAnnotationsAreReadAsBefore` (pages put back by undo; links and notes keep PDFKit's own classes; another application's stamp draws exactly as without the delegate), on macOS and in the hosted iOS suite; `PicturesAndPaperTests.testPictureReadFromAFileIsSelectedByATapAndStaysUnderTheInk` checks a reopened session's picture is Graphite's annotation.
- Not checked: on the iPad itself, and on the Mac app's PDF view, which uses the same code.
- Older builds: a picture turned by hand on a PDF page is now stored under `GraphitePictureV2`; upright pictures stay under `GraphitePictureV1`, and turning one back upright returns it there. `PDFPictureTests.testATurnedPictureIsStoredUnderItsOwnKeyAndOlderFilesAreStillRead` checks the keys, that a turned picture under the old key (as written on 2026-10-11) still reads turned, and that the new key survives a save that inserts a page. Main's own code (62e023b, the same picture code as the owner's main) was run in a scratch checkout on a PDF written by this branch: it lists only the upright picture, shows the turned one turned (red above blue where it stands, white where an upright one would be), and cannot select or move it. After main moved its upright picture and saved, this branch read the turned picture back with its frame and angle; after main inserted a page and saved, the turned picture was still a visible stamp but had lost Graphite's keys, so it can no longer be arranged (main drops keys it does not know on a page-tree rewrite, as it already did for the angle under the old key).
- Names: the quarter turn clockwise was the button "Turn Image" and the step "Turn Image", while rotating by the handle was the step "Rotate Image". Following Preview's Rotate Right and the PDF pages' own Rotate Right, the button and its step are now "Rotate Image Right" (`rotatePictureRight`, `rotateSelectedPictureRight`), and the handle's step stays "Rotate Image" (`rotatePicture(_:to:)`). `PDFPictureEditingTests` (macOS) and `PicturesAndPaperTests` (hosted, 16) pass with the new names.
- The rotate handle at the top edge, not tried in the review: in the iPad simulator, a picture moved to the top of a PDF's only page had its handle drawn over the tab bar, where a drag did nothing (`top-edge-before.png`). The handle now stands below the frame wherever it could not be reached above (the view's bounds less its safe area, also as a scroll view scrolls); seen below the picture there and turning it by a drag. The help line under the page now says "the arrow handle" rather than "the handle above it". ## PDF ink undo after the page's ink is read back: 2026-10-10

Reported by the owner on their iPad: Undo on a PDF page answered "The ink on this page changed in a way this step does not know, so it was not undone." Cause, found by experiment in the iPad simulator: PencilKit stores a stroke's color and transform as 32-bit numbers. A stroke in a color made from a hex value, as the fixed bar's colors are, or a stroke the lasso moved, reads back from the page's stored drawing a little different from the stroke drawn (a red component of 0.8313725490 reads back as 0.8313725590), and `PDFStrokeFingerprint` compared those numbers at full precision. Undo then refused every step drawn before the page's ink was read back, which happens when a tab switch rebuilds the PDF view and when a page's canvas, released far from the page shown, is made again. Black ink and strokes the eraser cut read back exactly. Locations and creation dates read back exactly too.

Fix: the fingerprint keeps a stroke's color and transform at the precision PencilKit stores them with (`PDFStrokeFingerprint`, GraphiteApple). A recolored or moved stroke still counts as changed.

Checked in iOS 27.0 simulators (iPad Pro 11-inch (M5) and iPhone 17 Pro), Xcode 27.0:

- `PDFInkHistoryTests.testColoredAndMovedStrokesAreUndoneAfterThePageInkIsReadBackFromTheFile` draws a colored stroke and a moved one on a page, switches to another tab and back, undoes and redoes, then scrolls far enough to release the page's canvas and undoes both. `testColoredAndMovedStrokesKeepTheirPlaceInTheHistoryThroughTheStoredDrawing` undoes a change against the drawing read back from its stored form, and checks that reading it back is no change while a recolored stroke still is. Without the fix both failed on the iPad simulator with the owner's message; the other seven tests of the class, which draw black ink at whole-number points, passed.
- With the fix, the whole `GraphiteIntegrationTests` target passed: 194 tests on the iPad simulator, and on the iPhone simulator 191 with 3 Canvas tests skipped, as they are at a phone's width. The changed file is built only for UIKit, so the macOS suite does not run it and was not run again.

## Apple Pencil double-tap in the drawing editor: 2026-10-10

Reported by the owner on their iPad: a double-tap of Apple Pencil in the drawing editor closed the editor and opened it again. Cause, found by reading the code: a Pencil gesture reaches every `UIPencilInteraction` in the window, and the note's text view stays in the window under the full-screen drawing editor. The note's handler checked only that its text view had a window, so it answered too and asked for the drawing at the note's cursor. The editor is presented with `fullScreenCover(item:)`, so the new request closed it and presented another, without the strokes not yet saved. Neither tool layout nor the kind of drawing changes this: the note's handler runs the same with the floating palette and the fixed bar, for a new drawing and for a drawing opened from its embed.

Fix: the note answers a double-tap or squeeze only when no screen is presented over it (`NativeMarkdownEditor.Coordinator`), and `WorkspaceModel.beginDrawingAtCursor` does nothing while a drawing is open. The editor's own answer is unchanged: Apple's palette, or the fixed bar's `PencilGestureReceiver`, following Settings › Apple Pencil.

Checked: the change was written in a Linux container without Xcode, then built and run on a Mac the same day (Xcode 27.0, macOS 27.0.1) in iOS 27.0 simulators, an iPad Pro 11-inch (M5) and an iPhone 17 Pro. With only the two new hosted tests on top of the code before the fix, both failed on the iPad simulator as the report describes: the note stayed in the window under the editor, its double-tap replaced the open drawing's request, and the editor closed and was presented again; the other 14 tests of `PencilToolsTests` passed. With the fix, all 16 passed on both simulators. `swift test` on macOS ran 1,616 tests (792 core, 137 index, 191 Apple, 496 UI) with the measurement test skipped as before and one failure, `UiEditorMacEditorTests.testHeadingJumpWaitsForAWindowThenSelectsAndScrollsToTheHeading`, in the Mac editor, which this change does not touch, while a simulator build ran at the same time; on its own it passed three times out of three. The two new tests:

- `testPencilDoubleTapInTheDrawingEditorKeepsItOpenWithItsStrokes` presents the drawing editor over the note as `GraphiteRootView` does, opens it with the note's double-tap handler, draws a stroke, then gives the note the double-tap and squeeze again, as UIKit does. The request, the presented editor and the stroke stay. With the fixed bar (regular width only), the bar's receiver switches to the eraser and the editor stays. A drawing embedded in the note opens as itself and keeps a stroke added to it. The test first checks that the note is still in the window under the editor, which is what the cause depends on.
- `testANoteLeavesPencilGesturesToAScreenOverItAndNeverReplacesAnOpenDrawing`: with a screen presented over the note, the handlers start nothing; while a drawing is requested but its editor is not on screen yet, a double-tap does not replace it; with neither, the double-tap starts a drawing.

Checked by the owner on their iPad Pro 11-inch (3rd generation), iPadOS 27.0, with a Release build of this change and the fixed bar: a double-tap of Apple Pencil while drawing in a note's drawing no longer closes it and opens it again; a Debug build made the same day before the change still did. ## Not implemented yet

Obsidian community plugin installation and execution are not implemented. The future target is to run existing plugin packages obtained from Obsidian's community directory, GitHub releases, or other sources without a Graphite-specific version, preserving their settings and file formats. Compatibility has not been validated. New plugin integrations and runtime implementation are deferred until the app feels premium; UI and interaction polish, Pencil quality, essential handwriting tools, and daily Obsidian workflows take priority. See `OBJECTIVE.md` for the scope and `Docs/Architecture.md` for runtime constraints.

A full comparison with Obsidian mobile, ranked for studying on an iPad, is in `Docs/Obsidian-mobile-gaps.md`. The defects it found in existing features are fixed (see above). The main remaining items:

- Bases Kanban settings (column width, hiding empty columns, cover images, column order, a new note from a column) and moving cards between folder columns: Obsidian 1.14's keys for them are not documented. Map styles (vector tiles) in `mapTiles` show Apple's map with a notice. `html()` shows no images or frames.
- Choosing a different format for an existing drawing (use Export a Copy).
- Native text selection handles, magnifier, Look Up and Translate on PDFs while annotating: a long press selects a word and dragging extends it, Graphite's own implementation. Outside annotation, PDFKit's native selection is used.
- Undo for PDF bookmark changes (toggle the bookmark again). A PDF's undo history ends when its hidden session is released or the file reopens after an external change, and is not kept across launches.
- Embeds of one PDF now coordinate: before an embed annotates, the other embeds and a PDF view showing the file are saved. The PDF view does not do the same before its own edits, so editing a PDF in its view while an embed of it has unsaved annotations can still end in a conflict.
- Filling PDF forms: form fields are shown read-only.
- Video keeps no sound while Graphite is off screen, and pauses while Graphite shares the screen with another app, which needs Apple's multitasking-camera entitlement. One fixed video quality. A player already showing an audio or video file does not reload after a conflict version replaces it.
- Hidden tabs keep their note sessions open; only hidden PDFs beyond the most recently visible one are released. Note editors are bounded (three kept hidden), sessions are not.
- Rendering SVG files made by other applications inside the reading view (they open with the system preview instead).
- Tabs: undo history survives switching tabs, moving a tab to the other side, and reading view for the three most recently hidden notes; beyond them, after a memory warning, and when a tab opens another file, the editor is rebuilt without its history (the note's text, cursor and scroll position are kept); a file is open in at most one tab, so "Open in New Tab" on a file already open shows its tab; tab history is not saved between launches; tabs stack at most two sides, left and right; no saved named workspaces. Reading view now retains its vertical offset for the open session, with the limitations recorded above.
- Opening a second vault in its own window, as Obsidian does on the desktop; one vault is open at a time.
- Bases: a date Range and durations are shown as exact parts, where Obsidian humanizes them ("a month"); `date("not a date")` and `duration("soon")` are errors, where Obsidian gives an empty value.
- Renaming or deleting a vault's folder from Graphite (do it in Files, then open the folder again).
- Per-vault Graphite preferences: which core plugins are on, the editor defaults and the Colors palette apply to every vault on the device, where Obsidian keeps core plugin choices per vault.
- 10,000 and 100,000 file measurements on hardware.
