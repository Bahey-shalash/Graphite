# Graph view: TODO

Created: 2026-10-21.

Priority: high. The owner asked for it on 2026-10-21, looking at his vault's graph on the Mac: "improve drastically, make it more accessible, not through view then graph view, make tags available and make it just look nicer".

Status: sections 1 and 2 on the branch `graph-view-ways-in` (2026-10-22). Section 2: tags shown by default with a # toggle over the graph, chosen with a tap to bring out their notes, named from further out, counted in the corner; nested tags stay the written tags, as in Obsidian. Section 1, all but the iPad's one-tap button: the graph opens as a tab, notes open beside it, a button in the file list, ⇧⌘G and ⌥⌘G, the local graph from a note's More menu and back to the whole graph centered on the note, Show in Graph on tags and folders, and the Guide. Before that: the graph itself ships and is correct. `LinkGraph`, `GraphLinkResolver` and `GraphLayout` build it, `VaultGraphView` (then `VaultGraphScreen`), `LocalGraphPanel` and `GraphCanvas` draw it, `LinkGraphTests`, `VaultGraphTests` and `GraphLinkResolverTests` cover it, and it was checked on the iPad on 2026-09-24 (`Coverage.md`). Nothing in this file is built yet: it is about reaching the graph, tags in it, how it looks, and how it behaves on a vault the owner's size.

## Request

Today the whole-vault graph is reached through View › Graph View on the Mac or "Graph view: Open graph view" in the command palette, and it opens as a modal: a page-sized sheet on the Mac, a full-screen cover on iPadOS (`GraphiteRootView`, `workspace.isGraphPresented`). It closes as soon as a note is opened from it. Tags are off by default and their toggle sits inside the Graph Settings popover. The drawing is plain: gray notes, links at 18 percent, names only from a zoom of 1.4, no colored groups.

The owner wants it one tap away, tags there without hunting for them, and a graph that is good to look at on a vault of about 4,700 notes.

## Decisions

- It stays Obsidian's graph: the same links, filters and forces, read from the same index. This file is about the way in, tags and the look, not a second graph.
- Its settings stay on the device (`GraphSettingKey` in `WorkspaceGraph.swift`), as Obsidian keeps them in its workspace. Nothing new is written into the vault.
- Groups are Obsidian's groups: a search and a color, not a new file format.
- The look follows Graphite's theme and accent colors, not Obsidian's palette.

## 3. A graph worth looking at

Issue: [#66](https://github.com/Graphite-Note/Graphite/issues/66)

- [x] Obsidian's groups: colored groups from a search, added and removed in the graph's settings and kept per vault with the other graph settings; every node a group matches takes its color. The owner's vault is mostly courses, so a group per course is the first thing he will make.
  Done 2026-10-22: Graph Settings › Groups (`GraphColorGroupsSection`) adds a group in the next color of Graphite's palette, edits its color and its search, and removes it. The search is read as the filter reads it (`tag:#name` with the tags nested under it, `path:`, words of the path). `LinkGraph.colorGroupIndexes(for:)` (Core) gives each note or attachment the first group whose search it matches, on the whole vault's graph, so `tag:` words count while tags are hidden; tags and links without a file keep their colors, a group without words colors nothing, and the open note keeps the accent. The groups are the vault's, on the device under `graphColorGroups.<vault identifier>` (`GraphColorGroupStore`, one for every window), never in the vault; the stored list writes back the fields and elements it does not read, a value that is not a list is kept aside rather than written over, and removing the vault from the list forgets them. Decided: one set of groups per vault colors both graphs (Obsidian keeps the local graph's groups apart; a course's color should be the same in both), and Obsidian's own `.obsidian/graph.json` groups are not read, as nothing of the graph's is read from or written to the vault. Tests: `GraphColorGroupTests` (8), `GraphColorGroupStoreTests` (6), `GraphSettingKeyTests` (the keys), `WorkspaceDeviceStateTests.testRemovingAVaultFromTheListForgetsEveryOtherThingKeptForItOnTheDevice` (fails without the removal line). Seen in the iPad simulator: a group `path:Voyages/` turned the five travel notes red as it was typed, in the whole graph and in Paris's local graph, and they were still red after a relaunch, the group kept in the app's preferences. Not seen on the Mac by hand.
- [x] The display settings Obsidian has and Graphite lacks, each beside Arrows: node size, link thickness, and the zoom at which names appear (`GraphCanvas.labelScale`, 1.4 today).
  Done 2026-10-22: Graph Settings › Display has Names From Zoom (Obsidian's text fade threshold, 0.3 to 3, 1.4 by default), Node Size (0.25 to 3 times) and Link Thickness (0.25 to 4 times) beside Arrows, with Restore Defaults; they are kept on the device with the other settings (`graphNameScale`, `graphNodeSize`, `graphLinkThickness`). `GraphDisplayOptions` (Core) carries them to the canvas as one value: notes show their names from the chosen zoom and tags from half of it; nodes and links are drawn at the chosen multiples, the hit testing and arrow tips following the node size through `GraphViewport.screenRadius(ofNodeRadius:)`, the link widths through `GraphViewport.linkWidth(isFocused:)`. The local graph keeps the defaults, as Obsidian gives it settings of its own, and still names every node of a graph of 60 nodes or fewer. Tests: `GraphDisplayOptionsTests` (3), `GraphViewportTests` (2 new: a node's and a link's size on screen), `GraphSettingKeyTests`, `GraphTagsTests`. Seen in the iPad simulator, on the Demo vault with 82 made-up course notes added (101 notes): Node Size and Link Thickness made the nodes and lines larger, and Names From Zoom moved right hid every name at the fitted zoom but the open note's, moved left showed them. Not seen on the Mac by hand.
- [x] Names fade in rather than appearing all at once, and never overlap: at the drawn scale a name is left out when it would cover one already drawn.
  Done 2026-10-22: a name begins to show at three quarters of its name zoom and grows to whole at it (`GraphDisplayOptions.nameOpacity(of:atScale:)`), so names come in as the person zooms; the open note's, the focused node's and its neighbors' are whole at any zoom. Names are drawn over the nodes, and `GraphNamePlacement` (Core) places them most important first (the open note, the focused node, its neighbors, then larger nodes before smaller), leaving out a name that would cover one already placed, with a grid of cells so the work grows with the names on screen. Each name is measured once per graph (`GraphCanvasModel.nameSize(ofNodeAt:measuring:)`). The fit leaves room for the names that show faintly at its zoom. Tests: `GraphNamePlacementTests` (5: a covering name left out, importance first, larger nodes then the first node among equals, touching and wide names, a name without a place), `GraphDisplayOptionsTests` (4, the fade). Seen in the iPad simulator, on the Demo vault with the made-up course notes: zooming in, the tags' names were whole while the notes' came in faint, then whole, and no name covered another at any step; before, every name showed at 1.4 at once, piled over its neighbors. Not seen on the Mac by hand, nor on a vault of thousands of notes (section 4).
- [x] The node under the finger or the pointer keeps its neighbors bright and dims the rest, as it does today, and the open note keeps a halo in the accent color so it can be found in a vault this size.
  Done 2026-10-22: `GraphEmphasis` (Core) decides what stands out: the node under the pointer, the one dragged or the chosen tag keeps itself, its neighbors and its links bright and in the accent, the rest steps back, as before; the open note now never steps back. The open note is drawn last, over a soft glow and a ring in the accent (`GraphCanvas.haloRadii(ofNodeRadius:)`), whose ring keeps a 9-point radius when the node is a dot far out. Tests: `GraphEmphasisTests` (5; the open note's case failed on the commit before, which pinned the old behaviour), `GraphCanvasTests` (2, the halo's size). Seen in the iPad simulator, on the Demo vault with the made-up course notes: Paris, the open note, kept its ring and glow at the fitted zoom; choosing #cours/analyse kept the tag, its twelve notes and their names bright and dimmed the rest, Paris staying bright with its halo. Not seen: the pointer's hover on a Mac or an iPad with a trackpad (the hover itself is unchanged).
- [x] Colors chosen for light and dark against Graphite's accent colors: links are black or white at 18 percent today, notes plain secondary, attachments amber, tags green, and a link without a file looks like a note although it is not one.
  Done 2026-10-22: `GraphPalette` gives each appearance its own colors: notes a quiet gray, attachments amber, tags green, links in the page's ink at 18 percent in light and 20 in dark, fainter while a node is focused; every node color has at least the 3:1 contrast WCAG asks of shapes on the light and the dark page (the old amber had 2.1 and the old green 2.8 on light). Against the accent: when its hue is within 45 degrees of the tags' green (a green or teal accent) tags turn purple, and when it is near the attachments' amber (amber, orange, red) attachments turn blue, so the open note and the focused links, in the accent, never look like a tag or an attachment; a gray accent changes nothing. A link without a file is a dashed ring and its name is in italics, so it no longer looks like a note. The arrows take the notes' gray. Tests: `GraphPaletteTests` (6: contrast for every preset accent in both appearances, hue clearance from every preset, the green and orange cases, the hollow ring, the links' strength, hues around the wheel). Seen in the iPad simulator on the Demo vault with the made-up course notes and an attachment and a link without a file: light and dark, the open note's halo, the amber attachment, the dashed ring beside the note that links to it, and with a green accent Paris green and the tags purple. Not seen on the Mac, nor with Increase Contrast.
